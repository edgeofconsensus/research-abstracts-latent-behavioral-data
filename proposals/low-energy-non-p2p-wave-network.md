# Low-energy wave network: feasibility study
Status: speculative engineering concept; no claim of free global connectivity.

## Aim
Explore open, publicly accessible infrastructure-mediated (non-P2P-required) communication using low-energy wave transmission. Separate acoustic ultrasound from electromagnetic RF; these are distinct channels with different propagation and safety limits.

## Model
Define centralized or shared broadcast/access nodes, a client receiver, and an explicit return path where bidirectional internet is required. Specify spectrum, licensing, modulation, antennas/transducers, range, obstacles, bandwidth, latency, bit error rate, duty cycle and joules per delivered bit. Energy and infrastructure costs cannot be assumed zero.

## First controlled experiment
Compare a permitted low-power RF link with an ultrasonic short-range link using identical payloads. Measure received signal, energy, throughput, reliability, interference and distance under documented conditions. Use legal frequencies/power and avoid unsafe ultrasound exposure. Do not transmit on restricted spectrum.

## Falsification criteria
Reject configurations failing minimum connectivity or energy targets; compare with Wi-Fi, LoRa and existing broadcast systems. Publish measurements and negative results under an open license.
